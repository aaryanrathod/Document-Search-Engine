Space exploration is the use of astronomy and space technology to explore outer space. While the study of space is carried out mainly by astronomers with telescopes, the physical exploration of space is conducted both by uncrewed robotic space probes and human spaceflight. Space exploration, like its classical form of astronomy, is one of the main sources for space science.

The first artificial satellite, Sputnik 1, was launched by the Soviet Union in 1957. This event marked the beginning of the Space Age and ignited the Space Race between the United States and the Soviet Union. The United States landed humans on the Moon in 1969 with the Apollo 11 mission. The first human in space was Soviet cosmonaut Yuri Gagarin, who completed one orbit of Earth in 1961.

Mars has been explored by numerous robotic spacecraft. The Mars Pathfinder mission landed a rover on Mars in 1997. NASA's Curiosity rover, which landed in 2012, has been exploring the Gale crater. The Mars Perseverance rover landed in 2021. Space agencies around the world, including NASA, ESA, Roscosmos, and ISRO, are planning future crewed missions to the Moon and Mars.

The International Space Station is a modular space station in low Earth orbit. It is a multinational collaborative project involving five participating space agencies: NASA, Roscosmos, JAXA, ESA, and CSA. The station serves as a microgravity and space environment research laboratory in which scientific research is conducted in astrobiology, astronomy, meteorology, physics, and other fields. The station is also the largest artificial object in space and the largest satellite in low Earth orbit.

Private companies such as SpaceX, Blue Origin, and Virgin Galactic have also entered the space exploration sector. SpaceX has developed reusable rockets, significantly reducing the cost of launching payloads into orbit. The commercialization of space represents a new era for humanity.
