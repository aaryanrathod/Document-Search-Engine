The ocean covers more than 70 percent of the surface of the Earth. It is the principal component of Earth's hydrosphere, and therefore integral to life on Earth. The ocean influences climate and weather patterns, the carbon cycle, and the water cycle by acting as a vast reservoir and by absorbing solar energy and carbon dioxide. As a result, the ocean helps regulate global temperature and weather, and it provides habitat to a vast diversity of species and ecosystems.

The ocean is divided into five major ocean basins: the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, and Southern Oceans. The Pacific Ocean is the largest and deepest, covering more area than all land on Earth combined. The ocean contains 97 percent of Earth's water, with the remaining 3 percent in freshwater sources such as rivers, lakes, and glaciers.

Marine life forms the base of most of the world's food supply. Fish, molluscs, crustaceans, and many other aquatic animals are consumed by humans globally. The ocean also generates more than half of the world's oxygen through the photosynthesis of phytoplankton and marine plants. The study of the ocean and its ecosystems is known as oceanography.

Human activity poses significant threats to the ocean, including overfishing, pollution, plastic waste, and climate change. Rising ocean temperatures lead to coral bleaching and the destruction of marine ecosystems. Acidification of the ocean due to increased carbon dioxide absorption threatens the survival of shellfish and coral species. Conservation efforts are essential to protect the ocean and the species that depend on it for survival.
